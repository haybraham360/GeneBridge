Hereditary hearing loss (GJB2)

A plain-language guide, shaped around what you told us.

You're exploring hereditary hearing loss because it runs in your family. This isn't a diagnosis. Only a genetic test can tell you whether you carry it.
Autosomal recessiveGene GJB2Hearing and vision

What it is

Hereditary hearing loss linked to the GJB2 gene is one of the most common inherited causes of hearing loss from birth. Hearing aids, cochlear implants and early support help children thrive.

How it's passed on

It's recessive: a child is affected only if they inherit a changed copy from both parents. When both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has the same chances: 1 in 4 affected, 2 in 4 carriers, 1 in 4 with no changed copy.

See it in the simulator

Being a carrier

One changed copy. Carriers have normal hearing and usually never know.

Having the condition

Two changed copies. Hearing loss, usually from birth, that ranges from mild to profound.

Could I be a carrier?

If a brother or sister has this kind of hearing loss, there is a real chance you carry a changed copy. Only a genetic test can tell you, and it's free and confidential.
